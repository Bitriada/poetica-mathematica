1 · The triad: machine, language, stage

The monograph's Chapter V says it in one line: "The Tribit is the machine; the Algebroid is its language; the Dimensoid is its stage." This project takes the sentence literally. The python/ directory is the Algebroid: the ultra-complex numbers UC = C[τ]/(τ²), the Fare Algebra with its square-free basis, and the spectral involution — every claim displayed on this page is checked there, 43 tests in all. The canvas above is the Dimensoid: the Fare–Riemann sphere S³FR, the geometric body the monograph attaches to UC. The controls on the right are the Tribit: one point of that sphere, the eight rotations that steer it, and the two gates that move it between value and trace.

2 · The Fare ladder: S¹ ⊂ S³ ⊂ S⁷

UC sits inside a family: the transit algebra T₃ lives inside UC, and UC lives inside the twelve-dimensional Transit–Fare Algebra T generated by i, τ, θ with i² = −1, τ² = 0, θ³ = 8θ. The monograph's geometric reading of this nesting is the Fare ladder: each level of the Bitriada tower is a sphere embedded in the next — S¹ ⊂ S³ ⊂ S⁷ — with the nilpotent τ and the fare generator θ adding new dimensions at each rung. This page lives on the middle rung: the 3-sphere of tribits. The bottom rung is the circle of traces you can see in green; the top rung, the 7-sphere of TFA, is the next project, not this one.

3 · The gates: you can remember, but only once

A tribit is z = v + τ·t: a value v = a + bi carrying the classical content, and a nilpotent trace t = c + di carrying the fare — the receipt of what the computation cost. The atlas gate 0/a kills the trace and reads out the value; the overdrive gate ω/0 lifts the value into the trace. They form a one-way switch: going overdrive-then-atlas returns your value intact, but going atlas-then-overdrive replaces whatever trace you had with a copy of the value — the original receipt is lost, and because τ² = 0 no trace can stack on another. Press the buttons and watch the point jump between the trace-bearing region and the value plane. Multiplying by τ does exactly what overdrive does: z·τ = τ·val(z) — the overdrive gate is multiplication by the collapsed fare.

4 · The critical line: |val| = |tr|

The spectral involution ιs(τₙ) = n1−2sτₙ acts on the Fare Algebra; at s = ½ it is the identity, placing value and trace on equal footing — the monograph's balance condition, and the reason the sphere is round at all (Remark 4.2.4.1a). On the unit sphere the balance locus |val| = |tr| = 1/√2 is drawn in violet: algebraically it is the Clifford torus, and it is exactly where the intrinsic norm ‖z‖τ = |val| + |tr| attains its supremum √2 (Prop. 4.2.4.7 — Cauchy–Schwarz equality). The balance bar in the panel tracks your tribit's approach to the critical line. That the balance torus is the Clifford torus is our identification, checked numerically, not a claim the monograph makes by name.

5 · The nadir: pure transit, square-free labels

The green circle is the nadir N = {τs : |s| = 1} — pure trace, zero value: the "pure transit" states, which the monograph reads as Turing tape configurations. It is also where the square-free integers live: each prime p is assigned an angle, a square-free n gets the sum of its prime angles, and coprime multiplication becomes angle addition — τ₂·τ₃ = τ₆ read as geometry. We place the labels by logarithmic height, Φ(n) = 2π·ln n/ln 211, the assignment under which multiplication adds angles exactly; the monograph does not pin the assignment, and ours is a documented choice. The collapse map φUC that squashes this whole circle onto the single τ of UC is a linear surjection, not a homomorphism: it records the presence of primes, not their arithmetic — φ(τ₂·τ₃) = τ while φ(τ₂)·φ(τ₃) = τ² = 0.

6 · What is verified

Everything the page shows, the Python core proves: the UC ring axioms and the Leibniz trace rule; the C₈ group law of the rotations and that they leave the value plane untouched; the gates' one-way behaviour and idempotence; the fiber structure, the nadir, the intrinsic metric and the √2 bound with its equality case; the Fare Algebra's coprimality rule; the spectral involution's identity at s = ½, its functional-equation symmetry ιs∘ι1−s = id, and Z(s) = ιs(Z(1−s)); and the angle-addition labelling. The badge in the header re-runs 35 golden vectors through this page's own JavaScript math core and compares against Python's answers — the two implementations must agree, and they do.

7 · Honest limits

Four things this toy does not settle. First, the monograph calls the value map a Hopf fibration while its explicit fibers fix the value (the amber circles); the classical Hopf fibers (the gray nested tori) are a different family — we draw both, and they are genuinely different. Second, Proposition 4.2.3.5's rotation matrix (two identical blocks, value rotating too) contradicts Definition 4.2.3.3 (value fixed); the two-block form is the grammar lifted to the TFA scale per Remark 4.2.3.3a, and both are implemented, labelled, in the Python core. Third, the square-free label angles are our instantiation, not the monograph's unspecified assignment, and the full 117-point lattice of Chapter I is not reproduced here. Fourth, the claims the monograph itself marks as Claim are the author's proposals; this project verifies their internal consistency and their agreement with classical mathematics, not the framework's physics. The T₃, T₆ and TFA rungs of the ladder are named in the Python core but not implemented — they are the next rung, not this one.
