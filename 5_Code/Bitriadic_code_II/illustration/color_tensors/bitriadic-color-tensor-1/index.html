1 · The twelve operators

Each color operator X lives in a commutative ring ℭ over ℝ with a nilpotent generator τ² = 0. It decomposes into two pools: X = val(X) + τ·tr(X), where val is the flow pool (light contributed) and tr is the absorption pool (the nilpotent record of wavelengths absorbed — recording the same absorption twice annihilates the record). The operators stand in a 3 × 4 matrix: three mixing regimes over four spectral channels, magnitudes the powers of two, so every assignment leaves a unique imbalance signature.

The named roles. R, B, G — the additive primaries. C, Y, M — the subtractive primaries. W₁ = R + C, W₂ = B + Y, W₃ = G + M — the metameric whites, each holding a different spectral string. W₄ — the additive white reflex: appears pure white; its zero trace records its additive origin. K — the subtractive black reflex: appears pure black; its zero value records its subtractive origin. Ω — the equilibrium operator: flow and absorption balanced at full magnitude, the full potential spectrum.

2 · The assignment under analysis

An assignment selects one operator from each column — one regime per channel — giving 3⁴ = 81 possibilities. Operators combine by component-wise addition; the total X = V + τ·T carries the flow V, the absorption T, and the imbalance Δ = V − T. Row I contributes +M to Δ, Row II −M, Row III 0.

4 · Color ⇄ tensor: the observer’s gate

Give the instrument a color. It decomposes the color into the four channel signals — red–cyan, blue–yellow, green–magenta (the chromatic columns) and the reflex (luminance) — then quantizes each onto its regime: Row I if the signal exceeds the gate, Row II if it falls below −ε, Row III (the mixed regime) inside the gate. The gate ε is the spectator’s fare: the compression that trades the continuum for the legible 40 / 40 / 1 split (Ch. IX: “the perfect split is purchased by a decision”).

Presets include the equilibrium grey (→ the witness) and pure white — watch how white enters the reflex column while its channels stay neutral: the tensor distinguishes two whites, reflex and additive, that no observer can tell apart.

5 · The ring calculator: addition and τ² = 0

Operators add pool-wise and multiply as (v₁ + τt₁)(v₂ + τt₂) = v₁v₂ + τ(v₁t₂ + t₁v₂) — the τ² term is annihilated. Multiply two pure absorptions and the product is zero: recording the same kind of history twice destroys the record. That nilpotency is the ledger’s law.

Metamerism. W₁, W₂, W₃ and Ω render the identical equilibrium grey yet carry different spectral histories — “different spectral distributions appear as the same color because the observer samples only a finite projection of the continuum.” The projection here is lossless at the level of the 81 assignments (all render distinctly) and lossy exactly where the monograph says it must be: at the cells, and in the shared Δ values (the histogram’s collisions — the price of the powers of two).

Additive–subtractive duality. The all-additive assignment (R+B+G+W₄) renders pure white; the all-subtractive (C+Y+M+K) pure black; and the map that swaps Row I ↔ Row II in every column negates Δ, pairing the 80 non-equilibrium assignments into 40 bright/dark pairs. The duality is an involution of the grammar.

The neutral axis as equilibrium. The witness is not a grey among greys: it is the state where every channel’s flow and absorption cancel exactly — “the grey that underlies every graphic program’s color picker… the ladder is the carry that makes ascent possible; the grey is the carry that makes color possible.” Section 2 places every assignment on that ladder.

The instrument’s conventions (how colors are rendered and quantized)

The monograph defines the algebra — the ring, the twelve operators, the mixing rule, Δ, the partition. To make it executable, this instrument fixes three documented conventions:

Cell identity colors. Row I/II cells render their exact primaries (R, B, G, W₄; C, Y, M, K). All four Row III cells render the same equilibrium grey — metamerism by construction.

Assignment rendering (the observer’s projection). With row signs sⱼ ∈ {+1,−1,0} and Δ = Σ sⱼ·Mⱼ: the ladder luminance l = ½ + Δ/30; the opponent chroma (a−b−g, −a−b+g, −a+b−g) from the three chromatic columns, projected to zero achromatic leakage; saturation gain 2·min(l,1−l)/3 — chroma vanishes at the ladder’s black and white ends; then clamp. All 81 assignments render distinctly under this rule.

The gate. A color decomposes as a = r − (g+b)/2, b = bl − (r+g)/2, g = gr − (r+bl)/2, l = (r+g+bl)/3; each chromatic axis quantizes to Row I / III / II against ±ε; the reflex column takes the luminance residual, normalized, against the same ε. The nearest rendered assignment (exact search over all 81) is reported alongside — when gate and projection disagree, your color sits between two ledgers.
