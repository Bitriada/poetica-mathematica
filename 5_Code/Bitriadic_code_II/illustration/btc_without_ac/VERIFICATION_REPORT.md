## Claim 1 -- VERIFIED (exact)

**Monograph reference:** 4.2.1, 6.6.2

**Claim.** UC = C[tau]/(tau^2): commutative, associative, unital, dim 4; tau^2 = 0; pure-trace elements square to 0; inverse formula (v + tau t)^-1 = 1/v - tau t / v^2

**Computed evidence.** 200 random Gaussian-rational elements x, y, w: all axioms hold identically in exact Fraction arithmetic; UC.multiply implements the Leibniz rule val1*val2 + tau*(val1*tr2 + val2*tr1).

**Notes.** The FORMULA of Claim 4.2.1.2 is correct, but the one-line proof printed in the monograph multiplies by the wrong conjugate (a-bi-tauc-taudi)/(a^2+b^2); that product equals |v|^2 + tau*(t*conj(v) - v*t), not |v|^2. Typos in proofs, not in statements, throughout.

**Claim.** Each D_k is 'homeomorphic to S^3_FR with the same extrinsic volume' via phi_k

It is not measure-preserving as a map of measure spaces: vol(D_k) = 0.3334 but the labeled image has volume 1 -- a scaling factor of 3.000; the pushforward of the CONDITIONAL measure is uniform.

**Notes.** This is the load-bearing subtlety. The three 'virtual spheres' phi_k(D_k) coincide with S^3_FR as SETS; they are three provenance-labeled copies. 'Multiplication 1 -> 3' is label bookkeeping -- exactly the monograph's spectator-relative reading, here made sharp. The phrase 'same extrinsic volume' also conflicts with sec. 6.6.3's own Vol(D) = (1/3) Vol(S^3_FR).

## Where the Axiom of Choice enters (sec. 6.6.2)

The F2-level decomposition is constructive and choice-free, and it transfers constructively to any SINGLE explicitly chosen orbit. AC enters exactly one place: the sphere is the disjoint union of uncountably many F2-orbits, and the proof must pick one representative from each orbit to copy the 4-piece pattern onto the whole sphere. By non-amenability (Folner ratio -> 2) there is no measurable way to make those selections, so AC is invoked to assert a transversal exists 'without providing a method' -- the monograph's phrasing. The UC decomposition of sec. 6.6 needs no such step: the domains are given by an explicit O(1) angle test.
