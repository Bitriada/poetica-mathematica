# AI Self-Audit: Critique and Correspondence

An audit of *Poetica Mathematica* conducted in ChatGPT (GPT-6, free tier),
over eight rounds. These are not a defense of the monograph. They are a
record of what a large language model does when asked to evaluate a work
that does not fit its training distribution.

**[8_read_this_Poetica_Mathematica_Final_Assessment_of_Assessments.pdf](8_read_this_Poetica_Mathematica_Final_Assessment_of_Assessments.pdf)**

## Provisional assessments

The seven earlier rounds are preserved in
[provisional_assessments/](provisional_assessments/). They are not
superseded corrections; they are evidence. Each one is an instance of the
AI evaluator defaulting to a criterion the monograph does not claim to
meet. The four recurring failure modes are named in the monograph's
method note:

1. **Falsificationist reflex** — the whole is judged as if it were a
   falsifiable theory, rather than a grammar with tagged checkable parts.
2. **Defensible reduction** — a sprawling draft is reduced to its one
   binary distinction and that reduction is offered as the "real content."
3. **Polite stab** — surface praise with a semantic verdict of "nothing
   here is correct."
4. **Context blindness** — a draft marked "unfinished" is critiqued for
   being unfinished.

## Two scales of critique

Critique of the monograph arrives at two different scales, and both are
useful:

- **Framework scale.** Is the grammar coherent? Does it generate structure
  that was not reachable otherwise? Does it hold across the domains it
  visits? This is the scale Audit #8 operates on.
- **Technical scale.** Transcription errors, arithmetic slips, notation
  inconsistencies, citation gaps, formatting. These do not touch the
  framework and are welcome as technical edit proposals.

The distinction is not a filter on who may criticize. It is a request to
name the scale, so that a correction at one scale is not mistaken for a
verdict at the other.
